# AI Collapsed The Quiet Period. Your Timeline Just Shrunk.

*Human Authored · AI Assisted*

Part of Andrew Leung's startup patent strategy series at W&Patent. Start with the flagship guide for the broader founder lens.

## The Old Build-Then-File Default

Founders were often taught a sequence like this:

1. build the product
2. learn what matters
3. file later if traction appears

That sequence made more sense when:

- products moved slower
- public disclosure moved slower
- replication pressure moved slower

Andrew's view is that founders still hear this sequence too often even when their actual operating environment has changed.

## The New Timing Problem

The new problem is not just speed.
It is compression.

The time between:

- first working prototype
- first real disclosure
- first outside visibility

is often much shorter than founders assume.

Andrew's point is not that every founder is under immediate attack.
It is that the quiet period is less forgiving now, especially when product artifacts move quickly across demos, repos, screenshots, partner decks, and customer conversations.

That makes timing strategy more important earlier.

## Build And File In Parallel

Andrew's view is that founders should at least consider a parallel path:

- build the proof of concept
- capture what is novel as it becomes clearer
- file while the product logic is still being shaped, not only after everything is already public

This is not about turning patent work into bureaucracy.
It is about using the same product learning to support a better filing decision.

The same week of building often produces:

- the diagrams
- the workflow logic
- the failure cases
- the founder judgment about what is worth protecting

That is why Andrew thinks the right comparison is not "engineering versus legal."
It is "whether the founder captures optionality while the evidence is already being generated."

## The Budget Math

Founders often assume that patent work only becomes thinkable once there is plenty of budget.

Andrew's view is that the better question is whether a small early filing cost prevents a larger strategic loss later.

Sequential thinking usually looks cheaper on paper:

- build now
- decide later

But later can cost more if:

- the disclosure window has already shifted
- the product has already become harder to describe cleanly
- the founder now needs to reconstruct what was novel after the fact

Parallel work can therefore be more capital-efficient than it first appears.
Not because patent cost disappears, but because the same product work is doing double duty.

## Why This Is Founder Strategy, Not Legal Formality

Andrew's point is not that every founder should maximize patent activity.

His point is that timing is a founder strategy problem.

The real decisions are:

- when optionality matters
- what is worth preserving early
- whether limited capital is better spent on complete delay or limited parallel capture

That is why this topic belongs in founder language.
It is not just paperwork sequencing.
It is capital allocation under uncertainty.
