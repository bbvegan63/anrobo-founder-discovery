# NDAs Won't Save You. A Provisional Will.

*Human Authored · AI Assisted*

Part of Andrew Leung's startup patent strategy series at W&Patent. Start with the flagship guide if you have not read it yet.

## Quick answer

Provisional vs. NDA for founders: file first on the one enabling piece that matters, then talk with judgment. An NDA can help in narrower relationship contexts, but it is not your moat and it does not preserve the same filing optionality.

## VCs Don't Sign NDAs

Founders waste too much time trying to solve the wrong first problem.

The usual move is:

- draft an NDA
- ask for signatures
- worry about who saw what

But most investors do not sign NDAs for pitch meetings.
That is not scandalous. It is just how the market works.

Andrew's view is that founders should stop treating the NDA as the first protection milestone and start asking a better question:

What is the fastest, cheapest step that gives me real optionality before I disclose?

For many founders, that step is not a signature.
It is a provisional filing.

## The Grace Period Is Real But Not A Strategy

United States law gives founders some room after disclosure.
That is real.

Andrew's view is that it still should not be the plan.

Why:

- it leaves more room for later disputes
- it creates false comfort
- it does nothing to preserve most foreign rights

Legal room is not the same thing as strategic room.
If you can file before talking, Andrew's view is that you should treat that as the cleaner default.

## Foreign Rights Die First

This is the part too many founders learn late.

If you disclose before filing, the damage often shows up outside the United States first.
That matters if your market, manufacturing, or licensing future is not purely domestic.

Andrew's founder-language version is simple:

- if the idea is important enough to worry about
- and you might care about non-US rights
- filing first usually buys more freedom than talking first

That is the decision frame founders should use.

## What An NDA Actually Does

An NDA can be useful.
It is just not the same thing as a filing.

An NDA does this:

- creates a confidentiality obligation
- helps when the other side is actually willing to sign
- can matter in a narrower relationship context

A provisional does this:

- establishes an earlier filing date
- preserves more optionality
- lets a founder have more detailed conversations with less fear of accidental timing mistakes

Andrew's view is not that NDAs are worthless.
It is that founders often overrate what an NDA solves and underrate what a timely filing solves.

## File First. Then Talk Freely.

The practical founder takeaway is boring in the best way:

1. capture what is novel
2. file before the important conversation if the disclosure matters
3. stop making the NDA negotiation the center of the process

Andrew's point is that founders should spend less energy asking for permission to talk and more energy making sure they already did the cheap strategic step that lets them talk with more confidence.

That is why his default is:

file first, then talk freely.
