# W&Patent Defensibility Proof Note Design

> **Date:** 2026-05-20
> **Module:** `anrobo-founder-discovery`
> **Founder:** `wpatent`
> **Status:** Design approved and user-authorized to proceed

## Purpose

Draft the next W&Patent founder-facing proof asset in `anrobo-founder-discovery` before any public site implementation.
The asset should answer a real imported mixed-mode question:

`when should a founder choose patents, open licensing, or other non-patent defensibility strategies?`

The immediate goal is not to settle an ideology debate.
The goal is to give founders a practical decision frame that strengthens W&Patent's credibility and supports the existing startup patent strategy surface.

## Why This Exists

The first meaningful imported feedback from the mixed-mode pilot was a founder-list counter-question about anti-patent or open-license strategies such as Creative Commons, Apache, and GPL.

That matters because:

- it is evidence-led rather than speculative
- it exposes a real topic gap in the current W&Patent proof network
- it tests whether W&Patent can sound discerning instead of one-note
- it may grow into a larger citation-target page later, but it does not need that weight yet

The current execution guidance in `founders/wpatent/evidence/2026-05-19-next-moves.md` already points to the smallest useful next asset:

- a short founder-facing proof note on patent strategy versus open or defensive alternatives

## Design Goals

1. Answer the imported question directly in founder-facing language.
2. Keep the asset practical, not ideological.
3. Strengthen W&Patent's point of view by showing better judgment, not weaker conviction.
4. Tie the note back to `startup-patent-strategy.htm` so it supports an existing citation surface.
5. Keep the draft small enough to publish as a proof note first and expand later only if the topic keeps recurring.

## Non-Goals

- writing the public site page in this repo
- turning the note into legal advice
- building a full patents-versus-open comparison hub
- cataloging software license taxonomy in detail
- rerunning prompt evidence before a public asset exists

## Recommended Asset Shape

**Output type:** `ProofNote`

**Working title:** `When founders should choose patents, open licensing, or other defensibility strategies`

**Primary audience:**

- founders asking practical strategy questions
- mixed-mode channels such as founder-list follow-ups
- future readers who need a concise W&Patent point of view

**Relationship to existing assets:**

- support `startup-patent-strategy.htm`
- stay consistent with the Trust Chain framing
- act as a lightweight proof asset first, with room to expand into a dedicated page or comparison page later

## Content Structure

The brief should use the existing publishing-brief template and follow this structure:

### 1. Measured insight

State that the first imported mixed-mode feedback was a practical question about when open or non-patent strategies might be the better first move.

### 2. Distilled lesson

State that the right defensibility tool depends on what creates leverage:

- patents when exclusive control matters
- openness when adoption or ecosystem spread matters
- neither first when the business still lacks enough product, proof, or leverage clarity

### 3. Founder point of view

Make W&Patent's stance explicit:

- do not default into `patent everything`
- do not default into `patents are bad`
- decide from leverage, threat model, commercialization path, and trust surface

### 4. Draft body

The founder-facing draft should cover:

- a practical opening that reframes the question
- when patents are the right first move
- when open licensing or openness helps more
- when neither is the first priority
- how W&Patent thinks about the decision in practice
- a closing pointer back to the startup patent strategy page

## Tone And Guardrails

The note should be:

- practical
- founder-facing
- plainspoken
- strategic rather than academic

The note should not:

- use `anti-patent` as the main frame
- imply patents and openness are always mutually exclusive
- drift into a licensing taxonomy lesson
- sound like W&Patent is retreating from patent strategy
- turn into a broad culture-war comparison between openness and patents
