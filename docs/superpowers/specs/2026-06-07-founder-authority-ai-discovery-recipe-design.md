# Founder Authority Recipe Design

> **Date:** 2026-06-07
> **Module:** `anrobo-founder-discovery`
> **Status:** Approved design for a reusable founder-authority recipe for AI-era discovery

## Purpose

Document a reusable recipe for helping founder-led sites become easier for AI-era discovery systems to:

- identify
- ground
- interpret
- cite
- connect back to a real founder, company, topic cluster, and proof network

Informally, this can be described as an `AI SEO recipe`.
More precisely, this design treats it as a:

- founder-authority recipe for AI-era discovery
- founder trust-and-discovery recipe
- reusable AEO / GEO positioning pattern

The narrower phrase `AI SEO` is acceptable shorthand, but it should not be the only framing because the pattern is about more than ranking.
It is also about:

- entity clarity
- founder proof
- ecosystem anchoring
- service / content / proof alignment
- repeatable public trust

## Why Write This Down

The W&Patent repositioning work shows a pattern that is likely to recur for other cohort members, alumni, and founder-led expert sites.

That pattern is not:

- "make every alumni site look like W&Patent"

It is:

- "use W&Patent as the first worked example of a repeatable founder-authority pattern"

Writing the recipe down makes it easier to:

- reuse the same logic across future founder profiles
- distinguish what is general from what is founder-specific
- hand off repositioning work to other agents cleanly
- share the model later with founders or operators in a more compact format

This document itself is an internal design spec, so `docs/superpowers/specs/` is the correct location.
It does not belong in `outputs/publishing/`, which is reserved for founder-facing assets and public-facing drafts.

## Current Context

The need for this recipe emerges from the recurring pattern seen in founder-discovery:

- a founder has real expertise
- the founder has some ecosystem anchor or public context
- the founder's current site is partially legible but not consistently grounded
- the site's strongest assets are often fragmented across founder, company, proof, and services
- AI systems can sometimes interpret topic pages but still fail to name the founder or company clearly

W&Patent is the current worked example.
As of `2026-06-06`, W&Patent scored:

- strong enough on identity surface and intent fit
- weak on broad discovery
- fragile on branded grounding

That shape suggests a structural clarity problem more than a simple content-volume problem.

## Scope

This design covers:

- when the recipe applies

- automated site generation

## When This Recipe Applies

This recipe is a strong fit when most of these conditions are true:

- the business is meaningfully founder-led
- the founder has real expertise or operator judgment worth surfacing
- the site has topic relevance but weak or fragile founder/company grounding
- the business would benefit from clearer public authority
- there is some ecosystem, cohort, alumni, or community context that can serve as a real proof bridge
- the current site story is split or vague

This recipe is a weak fit when:

- the company should not be founder-led in public
- the founder does not want to be a visible authority surface
- there is no real proof, ecosystem context, or expertise to anchor
- the site is primarily a pure product surface with no expert trust layer needed

## Core Claim

The central claim of the recipe is:

`AI-era discovery improves when the founder, company, topic cluster, services, and proof network become one repeated, legible public story.`

That story should be:

- easy for humans to understand
- easy for AI systems to repeat
- supported by real external and internal proof

## Reusable Ingredients

The recipe has seven reusable ingredients.

### 1. Named Founder Surface

The founder must be visible by name, role, and area of judgment.

Questions this layer should answer:

- who is this person
- why should anyone listen to them
- what topics do they actually own
- how are they connected to the company

### 2. Clear Company Role

The company must be framed in a way that matches the actual business.

This often means reducing vague or outdated framing.
For example:

- product demo vs real advisory
- marketplace experiment vs actual service surface
- general website vs founder authority domain

### 3. Topic Ownership Cluster

The site should repeatedly reinforce a small set of topics the founder should be known for.

That cluster should be narrow enough to repeat clearly, but broad enough to support multiple pages.

### 4. Concrete Service Or Offer Wedge

A founder authority surface becomes stronger when it is not purely abstract.

At least one concrete offer should exist, such as:

- advisory
- assessment
- teardown
- virtual marking
- founder articulation support

This wedge should express practical value without becoming the whole identity.

### 5. Ecosystem Anchor

A founder often needs an external bridge that is neither self-assertion nor press fantasy.

Examples:

- cohort membership
- alumni status
- incubator affiliation
- founder community role
- advisor context

This should be real, supportable, and repeated consistently.

### 6. Proof Network

The public surface should include:

- guides
- case notes
- proof notes
- founder pages
- FAQs
- explicit answers

These should reinforce the same identity and topic story rather than scattering it.

### 7. External Signals

Discovery strengthens when the same founder/company/topic relationship appears outside the primary site.

Examples:

- LinkedIn posts
- ecosystem profile pages
- alumni pages
- collaboration references
- legitimate backlinks

## Standard Recipe Sequence

The recipe should usually run in this order:

1. identify the founder's actual expertise center
2. identify the site's current story split
3. choose the primary topic cluster
4. choose one concrete service wedge
5. identify the real ecosystem anchor
6. reposition the homepage and about layer
7. align services, guides, and proof pages
8. reinforce the same story externally
9. rerun discovery measurement after propagation

This is a positioning and proof sequence first, not a content-farm sequence.

## What Generalizes Well

The following parts are broadly reusable across founders:

- founder-led homepage positioning
- named expertise surface
- ecosystem anchor logic
- service / content / proof alignment
- offsite signal reinforcement
- repeated entity consistency
- post-publish measurement loop

## What Must Stay Founder-Specific

The following parts should not be standardized too aggressively:

- exact topic area
- exact service wedge
- tone and voice
- proof assets
- ecosystem wording
- founder biography details
- business model framing

This is why the recipe should be written as a pattern, not as a rigid site template.

## Success Signals

This recipe should be measured by more than broad generic retrieval.

Expected first improvements are often:

- clearer founder naming
- clearer company naming
- better founder-to-company linkage
- stronger branded grounding
- cleaner interpretation of what the site is for

Broader non-branded discovery may improve later, but it should not be the only success test.

## Risks

### Risk 1: Founder inflation

The site may overstate the founder's role or ecosystem position.

Mitigation:

- use only supportable language
- avoid inflated partner wording
- avoid invented authority

### Risk 2: Template copying

Teams may try to clone the surface without the underlying proof.

Mitigation:

- treat proof and relationship truth as mandatory
- keep the founder-specific layer custom

### Risk 3: Over-narrow ecosystem dependence

A site may become too dependent on a single cohort or community.

Mitigation:

- use the ecosystem anchor as a bridge, not a cage
- keep the secondary audience explicit

### Risk 4: Confusing this with pure ranking tactics

If the recipe is framed only as SEO, teams may chase keywords without fixing entity clarity.

Mitigation:

- keep the business-positioning and proof logic visible
- describe this as a founder-authority recipe first
