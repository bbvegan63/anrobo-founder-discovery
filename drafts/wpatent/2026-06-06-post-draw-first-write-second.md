## A picture is worth a thousand words at the USPTO.

Provisional patent applications are judged on what you possessed on Day 1. The USPTO doesn't care if your writing is beautiful. It cares if you can prove you had the invention — and drawings are the most direct evidence of possession.

## You can never claim more than you possessed.

A vague text-only description of "a bracket" gives you a broad claim — but collapse under prior art and you have nothing narrower to fall back to. A drawing of an L-shaped bracket with a 45-degree gusset gives you the broad claim *and* three narrower fallback positions.

**You can always claim less than you disclose. You can never claim more than you possessed.**

## Provisional is not about being perfect. It's about being possessed.

Stop treating your provisional like a term paper. It's not graded on prose quality. It's graded on whether a person of ordinary skill would agree you had the invention on filing date.

## Drawings help you prove possession.

A rough sketch proves you possessed that specific feature. A thousand words describing "a bracket" proves almost nothing when prior art shows up with any bracket at all.

## Drawings are the cheapest, most valuable evidence you can file.

A five-minute sketch today could save your patent two years from now. Formality doesn't matter. Possession does. Paper, whiteboard, phone camera — whatever captures the shape.

## An exit strategy. Escape copycat prior art.

| If you file with... | You can claim... | You cannot claim... |
|---|---|---|
| Text only — "a bracket" | A bracket (broad) | An L-shaped bracket with 45-degree gusset |
| Drawing + text of L-shaped bracket with 45-degree gusset | A bracket **OR** L-shaped bracket **OR** L-shaped bracket with 45-degree gusset | Nothing — you have all three options |

## Amendment allowed — derivable from drawings even when text is silent.

USPTO practice allows you to amend claims to add a feature if it's supported by the original disclosure. A drawing of that feature is disclosure. Even if your written description never mentioned the angled bracket — if it's in the drawing, you can amend to it.

## Draw first. Write second. Claim third.

1. **Draw first.** Sketch your invention from every angle. Sketch variations. Sketch edge cases.
2. **Write second.** Every line in your drawing gets a reference number and a sentence.
3. **Claim third.** Write broadly. Then look at your drawings and ask: *"If prior art forces me to narrow, what features do I have evidence for?"*

## Provisional is not a term paper. It's a chest of evidence. And the most valuable evidence you can put in that chest isn't your prose — it's your pictures.
