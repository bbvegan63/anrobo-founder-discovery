## AI collapsed the quiet period. Your timeline just shrunk.

In the old world, you had months between POC and patent. Build quietly. Test quietly. File when ready.

In the AI era, that quiet period is gone.

| Before | Today |
|---|---|
| POC takes 6 months | POC takes 2 weeks (AI coding tools) |
| You control disclosure | Users, beta testers, and API logs leak constantly |
| Competitors move slowly | Competitors scrape GitHub and Product Hunt daily |
| Patent attorney costs $15k+ | AI patent tools let anyone file for cheap |

The barrier to copying your idea is near zero. The barrier to filing a blocking patent on your approach is also near zero. Your competitor's AI patent tool can flag your GitHub commit as novel and generate a provisional in 45 minutes.

Your funding allocation has to change.

## The funding trilemma: build, patent, or sell? Pick two.

With a fixed seed round ($100k–$200k), you face three competing demands:

1. **Build POC** — engineering cost
2. **Provisional patent** — legal cost
3. **Market traction** — sales and marketing cost

In the old world, you'd skip #2 until #1 and #3 showed traction.

In today's AI world, skipping #2 means someone else files on your public footprint. Not because they're malicious — because their AI flagged your commit as novel and auto-filed before you did.

## Tandem filing: POC and provisional in parallel.

The solution isn't more money. It's better allocation. Run engineering and patent work at the same time.

| Week | Engineering (POC) | Provisional |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build core loop, no UI | Write 1-page invention description with engineer |
| 2 | Working prototype (ugly) | Draft claims around that specific prototype |
| 3 | Show to 5 friendly customers | Have AI patent tool expand description |
| 4 | Iterate on feedback | **File provisional** before any public demo |
| 5–8 | Keep building, now protected | Nothing — provisional is filed |

**Total legal cost:** $3k–$7k (DIY with AI + limited attorney review)
**Total engineering cost:** $30k–$60k
**Time to both POC + provisional:** 30–45 days

## The limited capital math: sequential loses.

Say you have $100k total.

**Sequential (old way):**

| Step | Cost | After 4 months |
|---|---|---|
| Build POC | $40k | Working prototype, no IP |
| File provisional | $10k | Filed, but POC hasn't been tested |
| Market testing | $30k | Feedback comes after filing |
| Remaining | $20k | Guessed on claims, no customer signal |

Problem: You filed before you knew what mattered. Your claims are guesses.

**Parallel (tandem filing):**

| Step | Cost | After 4 months |
|---|---|---|
| Build POC | $35k | Working prototype |
| Draft provisional alongside | $3k | Filed week 6 (parallel) |
| Market testing under provisional | $30k | Feedback *before* PCT decision |
| Refine claims from feedback | $2k | Claims match real value |
| Remaining | $30k | Both POC and smart filing done |

You end with more cash, better claims, and no guessing period.

## Parallel paths share costs — your engineer does both jobs.

The biggest cost of a provisional isn't the legal fee. It's the cognitive load of describing your invention clearly. Your engineer is already doing that work during POC.

| Resource | POC needs | Provisional needs | Shared? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engineer's judgment (what's novel) | Design decisions | Invention disclosure | Yes |
| Documentation of how it works | Code, specs | Written description | Yes |
| Drawings and diagrams | Architecture | Patent drawings | Yes |
| External legal fees | No | Yes (but minimal with AI) | No |

You're not adding a separate expense. You're capturing the same work in two forms: working code and a written description.

With AI drafting tools, that written description can be generated from your existing artifacts — PRDs, architecture diagrams, even detailed commit messages. The marginal cost is near zero.

## Keep a running lab notebook. That becomes your provisional.

This approach has one requirement: your POC must be documentable.

If your POC is a prompt chain that exists only as running code — no specs, no diagrams, no written architecture — you'll struggle to draft a provisional without stopping engineering.

The fix is simple: **keep a running lab notebook.** A markdown file of what you tried, what worked, and why it's novel. That file becomes your provisional draft. If you're building seriously, you should be documenting anyway.

## No public disclosure before filing. The one hard rule.

No Product Hunt launch. No Show HN. No conference demos. No press. No beta with strangers.

You can show investors under NDA. You can show customers under NDA. Everyone else waits until the provisional is in.

If you violate this, the entire strategy collapses. That's where most founders still screw up — not in the budget allocation, but in the discipline.

## Investors now expect this. A provisional signals capital efficiency.

Investors have seen too many AI startups get copycatted and patent-blocked. A provisional filed alongside the POC signals that you understand the game:

- You know the quiet period is gone
- You know AI tools make both build and file costs negligible
- You know how to allocate limited capital for maximum optionality

That's not paranoia. That's capital efficiency. And it's the only strategy that works when the timeline just shrunk.
