# What Every Patent Practitioner Knows But Won't Tell Founders

*Human Authored · AI Assisted · This is the pillar page for the series. Each section links to a deeper post.*

---

I'm a registered patent agent. I've worked inside the system. Here's what my colleagues won't say in a client meeting:

**Most startup patents won't survive a real challenge.** The examiner is underfunded and overworked. Your application will be examined by someone who has days, not weeks, to find the prior art that matters. You'll get your allowance. But validity is tested in litigation, not prosecution — and most founders never find that out until it's too late.

**"Patent pending" means nothing legally.** It's a marketing signal, not a legal status. But practitioners let you believe it matters because it keeps you engaged.

**Trade secrets are often stronger but harder to sell.** You can't put a trade secret on a pitch slide. You can't say "patent pending" in a demo. So the system nudges you toward filing — because filing is what the system knows how to sell.

**Continuations let you refile claims for years.** Most founders don't know this exists. You can keep your application alive, adapt claims as the market shifts, and build a portfolio without starting over. The system doesn't advertise it because it doesn't generate new filing fees.

**Your first office action is a template.** Examiners copy-paste the first rejection. The real negotiation starts at the second. Most founders see the first rejection and give up — which means they abandon perfectly viable applications.

This guide doesn't repeat the conventional advice. It tells you what the system doesn't want you to know — and what to do about it.

## Should you file a provisional? Yes. But not for the reason you think.

Practitioners say: "File to protect your invention."

What they won't say: The real value of a provisional isn't protection. It's optionality. It's proving you possessed the invention on Day 1 so you can talk to investors freely, without NDAs, without losing foreign rights.

**The short answer:** File a $60 provisional before you pitch anyone. Not because it stops copycats — it won't. But because it lets you stop negotiating NDAs and start building.

## How should you draft a provisional? Draw first. The text is secondary.

Practitioners say: "Write a detailed specification."

What they won't say: Your text is only as good as your drawings. The USPTO allows amendment to features shown in drawings even when the text never mentioned them. A five-minute sketch of an L-shaped bracket gives you escape hatches that a thousand words of "a bracket" never will.

**The short answer:** Draw first. Write second. Claim third. Your drawings are not decoration — they're your strongest evidence of possession.

**The full argument:** [Draw First. Write Second. Claim Third.](2026-06-06-post-draw-first-write-second.md)

## When should you file, relative to building? In parallel. Not after.

Practitioners say: "Build your product, get traction, then come back for the patent."

What they won't say: That advice assumes no one is watching. AI patent tools can generate a provisional from your public GitHub commits in 45 minutes. And once you disclose publicly, your foreign rights are dead — permanently.

**The short answer:** Run POC and provisional in parallel. The engineering work IS the disclosure work. Total legal cost: $3k–$7k, not $15k.

## The unifying strategy

The system is designed to serve itself. Law firms bill by the hour. Examiners work under impossible quotas. Patent tools optimize for filing volume, not founder outcomes.

You need a strategy that serves you, not the system.

| The system wants you to believe... | The truth |
|---|---|
| You need an NDA to protect your idea | You need a filing, not a signature |
| Your specification should be dense legal prose | Drawings matter more than words |
| Build first, patent later (or never) | Build and file in parallel or lose foreign rights |
| Your first rejection means your application is weak | First rejections are templates. Push back. |
| Filing a patent is a $15k+ commitment | With AI tools, a provisional costs $3k–$7k |

File fast. Draw well. Build in parallel. Trust the system for what it gives you — a priority date, claim continuity, a 12-month runway. But don't trust it to tell you how to win.
